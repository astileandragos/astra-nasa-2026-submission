# What ASTRA uses from the NASA Arrhythmia report

Source: NASA Human Research Program, Evidence Report "Risk of Cardiac Rhythm Problems during Spaceflight" (approved for public release 12 June 2017), https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/evidence/other/Arrhythmia.pdf . Read in full on 16 Nov 2026 (8-page PDF, 41 pages of text).

## What the report says that ASTRA uses (section IV.A.4 "Exercise", plus IV.A.1, IV.D and IV.E)

| Fact in the report | Where ASTRA uses it | Label on screen |
|---|---|---|
| High-intensity interval exercise (near-maximal efforts alternating with low-intensity efforts) has been prescribed for astronauts on the Space Shuttle, Mir and ISS | Activity page, card "Example: mix hard and easy efforts" | ADAPTED FROM NASA (the "1 minute hard, 2 minutes easy, 4 to 6 times" numbers are ASTRA's example) |
| Early ISS crews did a monthly submaximal exercise test: 5 minutes of rest, then three 5-minute stages at 25%, 50% and 75% of pre-flight maximal aerobic capacity | Activity page, card "Example: a 20-minute easy-to-brisk session" | ADAPTED FROM NASA (the "you could sing / talk in sentences / a few words" feel words are ASTRA's; NASA used it as a test, not a workout) |
| NASA has limited exercise testing for medical and research purposes to 85% of pre-flight maximum, because of concern about rhythm changes at high intensity, except in controlled cases | Activity page, card "Do not go to your maximum on your own" | ADAPTED FROM NASA |
| ISS astronauts are encouraged to wear heart rate sensors to monitor exercise intensity and to log sessions; a heart rate log can only screen, an ECG is needed to confirm an arrhythmia; heart rhythm is not ECG-monitored during routine exercise | Activity card "Wear the sensor when you exercise"; the heart rate notification advice | NASA GUIDANCE |
| Astronauts follow a reconditioning program, typically in the first 45 days after landing; long-duration crews start with submaximal exercise | Activity card "Start slowly after a quiet period" (shown only after a quiet day) | ADAPTED FROM NASA |
| Evidence of rhythm problems in flight is mostly case reports; there is little proof that spaceflight raises the risk of life-threatening arrhythmias | "Why it matters" text of the heart rate notification | NASA GUIDANCE |
| Crew members may be slow to report symptoms because of the mission impact | "When to ask for help" text of the heart rate notification ("do not wait") | NASA GUIDANCE |

## What the report does NOT contain (so ASTRA does not claim it)

- It names no specific exercises (no treadmill, bike, squats). The "examples" are structures (rest then 25/50/75% steps; alternating hard and easy), not exercise lists.
- It gives no heart rate or SpO2 alert thresholds, no recommended weekly amount of exercise and no advice for people who are not astronauts.
- It is about rhythm problems. Potassium and magnesium (Apollo 15) are discussed as a possible cause, but later data do not clearly support it, so ASTRA gives no food advice from it.
- Weekly exercise amounts (about 2.5 hours a day, 6 days a week on the ISS) come from a different NASA document (OCHMO TB-031), not from this report.

## Wording

The cards are written for people without medical training. They are examples and ideas, not a prescription and not medical advice. Mission procedures and medical staff come first.
